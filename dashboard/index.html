What this number means — read before trusting it

Implied value is this board’s own claim about what a protocol’s launch would be worth if its record stayed as it is: implied = 8085 × TVL0.4049 × growth × maturity. The 8085 and 0.4049 come from least squares on the 94 launch-comparable live-token protocols in this one frozen snapshot (DefiLlama, 2026-11-14) — not from a rule anyone chose.

PROXY — read this as order-of-magnitude, not a price. The typical error is a factor of ~3.1× (leave-one-category-out median |log error| 1.13; median standard APE 81%). The mean factor error is 1,505% and the mean standard APE 689%, because a few protocols trade at 19×–400× their TVL or under 1% of it. It is not a valuation, not an FDV, and not a forecast of whether or when a token launches or what it would trade at.

Support is labelled per row. 79 of 178 rows sit in categories checked leave-one-category-out; the other 99 carry a sparse category support tag — fewer than 4 launch-comparable comps in their category, so the fitted multiple is extrapolated with no category-level check. That tag is a count label, not a calibrated probability — and four or more comps is not high confidence either. All 33 Risk Curators are tagged: their category has a single comp.

How the ranking works — read this first

Implied value = TVL × multiple(TVL) × growth × maturity. The multiple is fitted from this snapshot, not asserted: log(market cap / TVL) = 8.9978 − 0.5951 × log(TVL), fitted by least squares on the 94 launch-comparable live-token protocols in the snapshot (bridges, liquid-staking receipts and exchange tokens excluded — their TVL is not a launch). The multiple is clamped to 0.01–3.0, so a $5M protocol gets ×0.83, a $50M one ×0.21, a $500M one ×0.05 and a $5B one ×0.01. Growth is the 7-day TVL change clamped to −60…+120% (weight 0.5); maturity is 0.6 + up to 0.4 for age up to 1,095 days (unknown age counts as 365 days — an imputation, not a fact: listed_at is the day DefiLlama began tracking the protocol, “time tracked by DefiLlama; proxy only”, not necessarily its age or how long it has run with no token). The same function scores every row; no protocol name is ever special-cased.

The formula in power-law form. exp(8.9978) = 8085, so before the clamp the model is implied = 8085 × TVL0.4049 × growth × maturity, with TVL in whole US dollars. The fit’s target is log(mcap_usd / TVL_usd) regressed on log(TVL_usd); a comp enters only with mcap > 0, TVL > 0 and a recorded category, and a missing 7-day change counts as 0% while a missing listing date counts as 365 days of age (an imputation, not a fact). The coefficients come from least squares on the snapshot, not from a rule anyone chose; the fit’s centre is geometric (no smearing correction), which is why the errors below are reported on the log scale, as factor errors and as standard APEs — three measures of the same misses, defined below. The clamp never binds on this board: it would bind below $581K and above $8.4B, while the board spans $5.0M–$2.75B and the comps span $5.0M–$10.2B — the fit interpolates, it does not extrapolate past the protocols it learned from.

The multiple is a PROXY. A circulating market cap is not a protocol valuation and not an FDV, and an implied value is this board’s own claim about what a launch would be worth if the protocol’s record stayed as it is. It does not predict whether or when a token is issued, or what it would trade at afterwards.

Checked, not just fitted. Leave-one-out on the 94 comps (each protocol predicted from a fit on the other 93, refit per fold — the held-out protocol never enters its own fit): median |log error| 1.17, typical factor 3.2×, median factor error 223%, median standard APE 86% — against 1.51, 4.5×, 351%, 88% for the flat ×0.25 house model. Leave-one-category-out (each category predicted from a fit on all other categories, n = 59): 1.13, 3.1×, 210%, 81% against 1.39, 4.0×, 302%, 81%. On the median standard APE the full model does not lead: size-only’s leave-one-out median is 83.59% against the full model’s 86.28%, and leave-one-category-out is ~81% for all four variants (full 81.09% against flat 81.09% — practically identical, the full model a hair worse). Per-category multiples were fitted and tested too, and rejected: leave-one-out 1.60, worse than the flat multiple, because ratios inside a category still span orders of magnitude. The check tests generalisation across protocol categories on this one frozen snapshot — nothing more. It is not entity-disjoint validation: the snapshot carries no parent or issuer linkage, so a held-out protocol’s related products can still sit in the train set, and an entity spanning categories can still leak across them.

Two error measures, not one. The factor error (exp(|log error|) − 1) × 100 is the symmetric multiplicative miss: predicting half the actual is a 100% factor error. The standard absolute percentage error |pred / actual − 1| × 100 is the familiar one: predicting half the actual is a 50% standard APE. An earlier version of this board labelled the factor error “APE” — that was a mislabel, corrected here; both measures are now reported under their own names, for every variant and every category. The median |log error| is the headline; the typical factor is the median of exp(|log error|) over the predictions, computed directly — not exp of the median log error, which differs on even-sized samples.

One comp carries a version marker: Aave V2 (Lending, $90.9M TVL, $1.72B market cap, 19× its TVL). It is the only Aave product in the snapshot that records the AAVE token’s market cap — Aave V3 ($14.36B of TVL) records none — so the parent token’s market cap is attributed to one version’s TVL, a comparable mismatch. The fit keeps it (the classification rule excludes versioned candidates from the board, not versioned comps from the fit) and the sensitivity is reported: without Aave V2 the fit is intercept 9.2562, slope −0.6130 on 93 comps, leave-one-out median |log error| 1.1556 (was 1.1710), median factor error 218% (was 223%), median standard APE 85.05% (was 86.28%). Removing it and rescoring the same 178 rows moves every implied value down by 1.7–12.2% (median 4.1%) and changes 81 of 178 ranks by at most 4 places, while the top 10 — and the top 5 in order — are unchanged. That is fit stability on the same candidate set; the slope and the two LOO medians alone do not prove ranking stability, because they are measured on different samples (94 vs 93 comps).

Every added part improves the median |log error| and the median factor error at each step — on those two measures only, and only as a descriptive tradeoff, never as statistical significance and never as superiority on all metrics. On standard APE the picture is flatter: the leave-one-out median standard APE is 83–88% for every variant (best: size-only, 83.59%; full: 86.28%), and the leave-one-category-out median is ~81% for all four (full 81.09% vs flat 81.09%). On standard APE means the full model wins leave-one-out (532% vs 819% flat) but loses leave-one-category-out to flat (689% vs 516%). The size fit’s edge is a log-scale edge: it predicts the typical protocol better on the log scale, and it does not make the standard APE materially better. The mean factor error tells a tail story: 22 of the 94 comps miss by more than 1,000%, and four miss by more than 10,000% — rain’s market cap is 400× its TVL, bitway-earn 36×, aave-v2 19×, while concentrator’s is 0.13% of its TVL and obol’s 0.06%. The fitted line cannot reach either tail, and no variant tested does. The median is the representative number; the mean is kept here so the tails are not hidden.

Pooled vs macro. The pooled numbers above weight every prediction equally, so the big categories dominate. A macro average weights every category equally instead: leave-one-out macro median 1.59 (macro mean factor error 4,608%; macro median standard APE 266.61%) against pooled 1.17 (2,352%; 86.28%); leave-one-category-out macro median 1.12 (macro mean factor error 1,310%; macro median standard APE 76.60%) against pooled 1.13 (1,505%; 81.09%). The macro is worse on leave-one-out because the small categories (one to three comps) have the largest errors and count as much as the big ones.

Only categories with 4 or more comps are tabled: below that, a category-level check has too few points to mean anything, and rows in those categories carry the sparse category support tag instead.

Risk Curators — the board’s largest category, and its least checked

33 rows are Risk Curators (12 watchlist + 21 early) — the board’s largest category — and their category has exactly one launch-comparable comp (Api3), so no category-level check exists for them. Every curator row carries a sparse category support tag. Their TVL is the vaults they manage; the sealed snapshot has no parentProtocol field and no vault linkage, so the board cannot tell whether a curator’s TVL and its vaults’ TVL count the same assets twice. The smoke test the snapshot does allow: no two board rows share an exact TVL, and 7 groups of rows share a first name word (15 rows, for example a curator and its vaults) — reported, not resolved. Implied values are never summed into a total: correlated proxies are not an independent opportunity. Curator farm plans describe a deposit into a vault the curator manages, not an interaction with the curator and not eligibility for any curator token.

Coverage — the whole universe, not just the ledger. The snapshot holds 758 protocols: 342 record no token and 416 already trade one. Of the 342, the 40 watchlist protocols are all on the board (mandatory, all above the $50M floor); 92 more pre-token protocols sit above the floor but are not on the watchlist. This board covers the mandatory watchlist plus a below-floor extension only, so those 92 are omitted by the entry’s selection policy, not by a floor rule — the input rules say a protocol the list leaves out is no worse a subject. They are not in the ledger. Twenty of the 92 pass the visible classification check, but the snapshot carries no parent linkage, so they cannot simply be added (they may be parent-related) — a labelled blind spot, not a resolved one, and ledger parity does not prove the 138 early rows are parent-product-free either. The remaining 210 below-floor pre-token candidates are the ledger’s universe. The ledger (exclusion-ledger.csv, 210 rows) lists those 210 with a status per row: 138 are on the board as early rows and 72 are excluded by the classification rule (liquid-staking receipts, bridges, exchanges, versioned pools, and names carrying wrapped or staked) — so the ledger is 210 rows, not 210 exclusions. The 416 live-token protocols are all set aside (they have tokens, so they are not candidates); the Live view below shows the top 24 by market cap. The board is 178 rows: 40 watchlist + 138 early. Say a protocol records no token, never that it has none: DefiLlama’s record is not proof. The watchlist is a floor, never a ceiling.
