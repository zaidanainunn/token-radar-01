What this number means — read before trusting it

Implied value is this board’s own claim about what a protocol’s launch would be worth if its record stayed as it is: implied = 8085 × TVL0.4049 × growth × maturity. The 8085 and 0.4049 come from least squares on the 94 launch-comparable live-token protocols in this one frozen snapshot (DefiLlama, 2026-11-14) — not from a rule anyone chose.

PROXY — read this as order-of-magnitude, not a price. The typical error is ~210% (leave-one-family-out median); the mean error is in the thousands of per cent because a few protocols trade at 19×–400× their TVL or under 1% of it. It is not a valuation, not an FDV, and not a forecast of whether or when a token launches or what it would trade at.

Confidence is labelled per row. 79 of 178 rows sit in categories checked leave-one-family-out; the other 99 carry a LOW CONFIDENCE tag — fewer than 4 launch-comparable comps in their category, so the fitted multiple is extrapolated with no family-level check. How the ranking works — read this first

Implied value = TVL × multiple(TVL) × growth × maturity. The multiple is fitted from this snapshot, not asserted: log(market cap / TVL) = 8.9978 − 0.5951 × log(TVL), fitted by least squares on the 94 launch-comparable live-token protocols in the snapshot (bridges, liquid-staking receipts and exchange tokens excluded — their TVL is not a launch). The multiple is clamped to 0.01–3.0, so a $5M protocol gets ×0.83, a $50M one ×0.21, a $500M one ×0.05 and a $5B one ×0.01. Growth is the 7-day TVL change clamped to −60…+120% (weight 0.5); maturity is 0.6 + up to 0.4 for age up to 1,095 days (unknown age counts as 365). The same function scores every row; no protocol name is ever special-cased.

The formula in power-law form. exp(8.9978) = 8085, so before the clamp the model is implied = 8085 × TVL0.4049 × growth × maturity, with TVL in whole US dollars. The fit’s target is log(mcap_usd / TVL_usd) regressed on log(TVL_usd); a comp enters only with mcap > 0, TVL > 0 and a recorded category, and a missing 7-day change counts as 0% while a missing listing date counts as 365 days of age. The coefficients come from least squares on the snapshot, not from a rule anyone chose; the fit’s centre is geometric (no smearing correction), which is why the errors below are reported on the log scale and as percentages. The clamp never binds on this board: it would bind below $581K and above $8.4B, while the board spans $5.0M–$2.75B and the comps span $5.0M–$10.2B — the fit interpolates, it does not extrapolate past the protocols it learned from.

The multiple is a PROXY. A circulating market cap is not a protocol valuation and not an FDV, and an implied value is this board’s own claim about what a launch would be worth if the protocol’s record stayed as it is. It does not predict whether or when a token is issued, or what it would trade at afterwards.

Checked, not just fitted. Leave-one-out on the 94 comps (each protocol predicted from a fit on the other 93, refit per fold — the held-out protocol never enters its own fit): median |log error| 1.17 (median error 223%) against 1.51 (351%) for the flat ×0.25 house model. Leave-one-family-out (each category predicted from a fit on all other categories, n = 59): 1.13 (210%) against 1.39 (302%). Per-category multiples were fitted and tested too, and rejected: leave-one-out 1.60, worse than the flat multiple, because ratios inside a category still span orders of magnitude. The check tests generalisation across protocol families on this one frozen snapshot — nothing more.

Every added part improves the median — the typical protocol is predicted better at each step. The mean APE tells a different story and is reported as-is: it stays in the thousands of per cent for every variant, and on leave-one-family-out the full model’s mean (1,505%) is worse than the flat model’s (1,279%) even as its median is better. The mean is a tail story: 22 of the 94 comps miss by more than 1,000%, and four miss by more than 10,000% — rain’s market cap is 400× its TVL, bitway-earn 36×, aave-v2 19×, while concentrator’s is 0.13% of its TVL and obol’s 0.06%. The fitted line cannot reach either tail, and no variant tested does. The median is the representative number; the mean is kept here so the tails are not hidden.

Pooled vs macro. The pooled numbers above weight every prediction equally, so the big families dominate. A macro average weights every family equally instead: leave-one-out macro median 1.59 (macro mean APE 4,608%) against pooled 1.17 (2,352%); leave-one-family-out macro median 1.12 (macro mean APE 1,310%) against pooled 1.13 (1,505%). The macro is worse on leave-one-out because the small families (one to three comps) have the largest errors and count as much as the big ones.

Risk Curators — the board’s largest category, and its least checked

33 rows are Risk Curators (12 watchlist + 21 early) — the board’s largest category — and their category has exactly one launch-comparable comp, so no family-level check exists for them. Every curator row carries a LOW CONFIDENCE tag. Their TVL is the vaults they manage; the sealed snapshot has no parentProtocol field and no vault linkage, so the board cannot tell whether a curator’s TVL and its vaults’ TVL count the same assets twice. The smoke test the snapshot does allow: no two board rows share an exact TVL, and 7 groups of rows share a first name word (15 rows, for example a curator and its vaults) — reported, not resolved. Curator farm plans describe a deposit into a vault the curator manages, not an interaction with the curator and not eligibility for any curator token.

Coverage — the whole universe, not just the ledger. The snapshot holds 758 protocols: 342 record no token and 416 already trade one. Of the 342, the 40 watchlist protocols are all on the board (mandatory, all above the $50M floor); 92 more pre-token protocols sit above the floor but are not on the watchlist and are excluded by the floor rule — they are not in the ledger; the remaining 210 below-floor pre-token candidates are the ledger’s universe. The ledger (exclusion-ledger.csv, 210 rows) lists those 210 with a status per row: 138 are on the board as early rows and 72 are excluded by the classification rule (liquid-staking receipts, bridges, exchanges, versioned pools, and names carrying wrapped or staked) — so the ledger is 210 rows, not 210 exclusions. The 416 live-token protocols are all set aside (they have tokens, so they are not candidates); the Live view below shows the top 24 by market cap. The board is 178 rows: 40 watchlist + 138 early. Say a protocol records no token, never that it has none: DefiLlama’s record is not proof. The watchlist is a floor, never a ceiling.
